Kitchen · 3 minute read

The quiet work of a thousand layers.

A croissant looks effortless on the plate. At the bench, it is a conversation between cold butter, gentle hands, and a little patience.

We begin with a block of cultured butter and a dough that has had a good night's rest. The butter should bend without breaking; the dough should feel cool and calm. If either one gets too warm, we stop and give it a minute.

Fold, rest, repeat

Each turn folds butter and dough into another set of thin, delicate sheets. The number matters less than keeping those layers distinct. We work in a cool room, use a light touch, and let the dough relax between turns instead of pulling it into shape.

By morning, the pastry has risen slowly and is ready for the oven. Steam lifts the layers; the butter gives them their colour and aroma. We bake until the outside is deeply golden and the inside is light enough to leave a few flakes on your shirt.

Best enjoyed right away

Take one while it is still a little warm. No plate required, though a coffee is never a bad idea.
