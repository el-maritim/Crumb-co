Why we give the dough the night.

A little time changes everything. Our bread begins with flour, water, salt, and a starter that prefers the slow lane.

At the end of each morning, we mix tomorrow's dough. It is a practical rhythm, but it is also a kind of trust: we do our part, then let the living culture get on with its work while the bakehouse sleeps.

Time is an ingredient

As the dough rests, natural fermentation builds aroma and a gentle tang. The texture grows more elastic; the crust gets a little more character. No two batches are quite identical, and that's part of what makes baking by hand worth doing.

We keep the dough cool overnight and check in as the morning starts. A warm day might ask for an earlier bake; a cold one may ask us to wait. The recipe gives us a map, but the dough tells us where we are.

Worth the wait

When a loaf finally leaves the oven, we let it cool before slicing. That last pause is the hardest one, especially when the crust is singing and the room smells like toast. Then it's ready for your table.

Next time you pick up a loaf, give it a little time at home too. A good knife, a thick slice, and something to share are all the equipment you need.
